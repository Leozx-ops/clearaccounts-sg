Every Singapore company is legally required to maintain proper financial records — not just to file taxes accurately, but as a statutory obligation under the Companies Act. Poor record-keeping is one of the most common reasons IRAS disallows expense deductions, and it is one of the most avoidable compliance risks for small businesses. This guide explains exactly what you must keep, for how long, and in what format.

1. The legal basis — IRAS, ACRA, and the Companies Act

Record-keeping obligations in Singapore come from three sources that work together:

  • Companies Act (Section 199) — requires every company to keep accounting and business records that accurately reflect financial transactions, and enable financial statements to be prepared
  • IRAS — requires records to support tax returns, expense claims, and capital allowances
  • ACRA — oversees corporate compliance and can inspect records during investigations

Outsourcing your bookkeeping does not remove your legal responsibility. Directors remain accountable for ensuring correct processes and access to evidence, even when bookkeeping is handled by an external provider.

2. How long must you keep records?

Your company must maintain proper records of its financial transactions and retain the source documents, accounting records and schedules, bank statements and any other records of transactions connected with your business for at least 5 years from the relevant Year of Assessment.

How to calculate your retention deadline: The 5-year clock starts from the end of the financial year — not the date of the transaction. For example, if your company's financial year ends on December 31, 2024 (YA 2025), the accounting records for that financial year must be kept until at least December 31, 2029.
Even if you close your company: Both ACRA and IRAS require that accounting records and supporting documents be kept for at least 5 years from the relevant transaction date or financial year end — even if the company has ceased operations or been struck off. If your company is wound up, you still need to keep the final set of records for 5 years after cessation.

3. What records you must keep

Income records

  • Sales invoices issued to customers
  • Receipts for payments received
  • Contracts and service agreements
  • Bank statements showing income received
  • Records of e-payments (PayNow, bank transfers, GIRO)
  • Point-of-sale records if you run a retail or F&B business

Expense records

  • Supplier invoices and purchase orders
  • Payment vouchers and receipts
  • Rental agreements and monthly rent receipts
  • Utility bills paid by the company
  • CPF contribution records for all employees
  • Director fee approval documentation (board resolution + bank records)
  • Travel and entertainment receipts with business purpose noted

Accounting records

  • General ledger and journal entries
  • Profit and Loss Statement for each financial year
  • Balance Sheet as at each financial year end
  • Bank reconciliation statements
  • Fixed asset register (if you own equipment, furniture, or machinery)

GST records (if GST-registered)

  • Tax invoices issued for all standard-rated supplies
  • Input tax invoices from suppliers (to support GST claims)
  • GST F5 returns filed with IRAS
  • Records of zero-rated and exempt supplies
  • Records of goods exported or international services provided

Payroll records

  • Monthly payslips for all employees
  • CPF contribution schedules submitted to CPF Board
  • IR8A forms (annual income statement for each employee)
  • Employment contracts and salary adjustment letters
  • Leave records

Payroll records and IR8A forms should be retained for 7 years per MOM (Ministry of Manpower) requirements — longer than the standard 5-year IRAS retention period.

Statutory records

  • Annual General Meeting (AGM) minutes or written resolutions
  • Board resolutions (especially for major transactions and director fees)
  • Register of shareholders and directors
  • Company constitution
  • ACRA filing confirmations (annual returns)

4. Digital vs paper — what IRAS accepts

Companies are strongly encouraged to keep their records using accounting software. Using an electronic record keeping system will incur lower manpower costs as the company would not have to manually track each and every business transaction.

IRAS fully accepts digital records — scanned invoices, PDF statements, cloud accounting software exports, and email confirmations all qualify. You do not need to keep paper originals if you have a clear, retrievable digital system.

GST-registered businesses — InvoiceNow requirement: From November 2025 (new voluntary registrants) and April 2026 (all new registrants), IRAS requires the use of InvoiceNow-ready accounting software that transmits invoice data digitally via the Peppol network. Manual Word or Excel invoicing is no longer acceptable for newly registered businesses. See our GST registration guide for details.

Best practices for digital record-keeping:

  • Use a consistent file naming convention — for example: 2025-03_SupplierName_INV123.pdf
  • Back up records to at least two locations (cloud + local drive)
  • Ensure records are retrievable within 24 hours if IRAS requests them
  • Do not delete digital records before the 5-year retention period expires

5. Real examples: Wei Ming, Priya, and James

Wei Ming — F&B business, IRAS audit triggered

Wei Ming's catering company was selected for a routine IRAS compliance review two years after filing his YA 2023 return. IRAS asked him to provide all sales invoices and expense receipts for the year under review. Wei Ming had kept paper receipts in a cardboard box — but several months' worth had been discarded during an office move.

IRAS disallowed SGD 18,400 in expenses that could not be substantiated, resulting in additional tax of approximately SGD 3,100 plus interest. The lesson was expensive: the cost of poor record-keeping was far higher than the cost of a simple digital filing system.

Lesson: Records do not need to be perfectly organised — but they must be complete and retrievable. Scan and store everything digitally from day one, regardless of how small the amount.

Priya — consultant, director fees not properly documented

Priya paid herself SGD 48,000 in director fees during her company's financial year. She processed the bank transfer and recorded it in her accounts — but did not pass a board resolution approving the fees, as required for the deduction to be valid for corporate tax purposes.

When her accountant prepared the Form C-S, she flagged the missing resolution. Without it, IRAS could challenge the deductibility of the fees. Priya passed a retrospective resolution before filing — technically valid, but better done at the time of payment. Going forward, she now passes a brief written resolution each time director fees are approved, takes 10 minutes, and costs nothing.

Lesson: Director fees require a board resolution to be deductible. This is a one-paragraph document, but it must exist and be on file. Many small company directors skip this step and create unnecessary risk.

James — e-commerce company, clean digital system

James set up his record-keeping system before his first transaction. He uses Xero for accounting, connected to his DBS business account. Every invoice and receipt is scanned and uploaded to a shared Google Drive folder organised by year and month. Bank statements are exported monthly as PDFs and stored alongside the corresponding invoices.

When James's accountant prepares his annual Form C-S, they access the Drive folder directly. The entire filing takes less than two hours because the records are complete and organised. James has never had a compliance issue in three years of operation.

Lesson: A simple, consistent digital system set up from day one is far less work than reconstructing records at year-end or during an audit. The investment is a few hours of setup — the payoff is years of stress-free compliance.

6. Penalties for poor record-keeping

Under the Companies Act, failure to keep proper records is an offence that can result in fines of up to SGD 5,000 or imprisonment for up to 12 months for the company and its officers. From a tax perspective, IRAS may disallow expense claims or capital allowances if they cannot be substantiated with proper documentation, potentially leading to a higher tax bill.

The most common real-world consequence: Most small businesses are not prosecuted — but IRAS will disallow deductions they cannot verify. If you claim SGD 20,000 in business expenses but cannot produce receipts, IRAS treats them as non-deductible. At 17% corporate tax rate, that is SGD 3,400 in additional tax — plus potential penalties and interest on the shortfall.

8. FAQ

How long must Singapore companies keep business records?
At least 5 years from the end of the relevant financial year, under both IRAS and the Companies Act. For payroll and IR8A records, the MOM requires 7 years. Even if the company closes, records must be kept for 5 years after cessation.
What records must a Singapore company keep?
Income records (invoices, receipts, bank statements), expense records (supplier invoices, receipts with business purpose), accounting records (ledgers, P&L, balance sheet, bank reconciliations), payroll records (payslips, CPF, IR8A), GST records if registered, and statutory records (AGM minutes, board resolutions, shareholder register).
Can Singapore companies keep records digitally?
Yes. IRAS fully accepts digital records including scanned invoices, PDF statements, and cloud accounting software. You do not need paper copies if you have a reliable, retrievable digital system with regular backups.
What are the penalties for poor record-keeping in Singapore?
Under the Companies Act, fines up to SGD 5,000 or imprisonment up to 12 months. More commonly, IRAS disallows expense deductions that cannot be substantiated — meaning you pay more corporate tax on income that was genuinely a business cost.
Do I need accounting software for my Singapore company?
Not legally required — but IRAS strongly encourages it. For GST-registered businesses, InvoiceNow-ready software is required from November 2025 (new voluntary registrants) and April 2026 (all new registrants). Manual Excel invoicing is no longer sufficient for newly registered businesses.
