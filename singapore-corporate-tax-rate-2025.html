Every guide on Singapore corporate tax starts with the same sentence: the rate is a flat 17%. That is true. But for most small companies — especially those in their first few years — 17% is not what they actually pay. Between the Start-Up Tax Exemption, the Partial Tax Exemption, and the government's annual corporate tax rebates, the effective rate for a small Singapore company is often 2%–8%. This guide shows you exactly how the numbers work.

1. The 17% headline rate — and why most companies pay less

Singapore levies corporate income tax at a flat rate of 17% on chargeable income. This applies equally to Singapore-resident and non-resident companies. There are no progressive brackets — every dollar of taxable profit above exemption thresholds is taxed at the same rate.

What makes Singapore's corporate tax genuinely competitive is not the 17% rate itself — it is the stack of exemptions and rebates that reduce what most small companies actually pay:

  • SUTE — Start-Up Tax Exemption for the first 3 years
  • PTE — Partial Tax Exemption for all other companies
  • Annual CIT rebate — government-announced relief for YA 2025 and YA 2026

2. Start-Up Tax Exemption (SUTE) — first 3 years

SUTE is designed to reduce the tax burden for newly incorporated companies in their most critical early years. It applies for the first 3 consecutive Years of Assessment.

Chargeable incomeSUTE exemptionAmount exempt
First SGD 100,00075%SGD 75,000 exempt
Next SGD 100,00050%SGD 50,000 exempt
Above SGD 200,0000%Taxed at full 17%

Source: IRAS — Tax Exemption Scheme for New Start-Up Companies (YA 2020 onwards)

Maximum SUTE exemption per year: SGD 125,000

SUTE qualifying conditions

  • Company is incorporated in Singapore
  • Company is tax resident in Singapore for that YA
  • Company has no more than 20 shareholders
  • At least one individual shareholder holds 10% or more of ordinary shares
Does not qualify for SUTE: Investment holding companies and property development companies are explicitly excluded, regardless of shareholder structure. If your company's primary activity is holding investments or developing property, you cannot use SUTE.

3. Partial Tax Exemption (PTE) — for established companies

Once a company has used its 3 years of SUTE, or if it never qualified for SUTE, it falls under PTE automatically. No application is required — IRAS applies PTE every year.

Chargeable incomePTE exemptionAmount exempt
First SGD 10,00075%SGD 7,500 exempt
Next SGD 190,00050%SGD 95,000 exempt
Above SGD 200,0000%Taxed at full 17%

Source: IRAS — Partial Tax Exemption (YA 2020 onwards)

Maximum PTE exemption per year: SGD 102,500

SUTE — New companies
  • First 3 Years of Assessment only
  • 75% on first SGD 100,000
  • 50% on next SGD 100,000
  • Max exemption: SGD 125,000/yr
  • Shareholder conditions apply
PTE — All other companies
  • Applies every year automatically
  • 75% on first SGD 10,000
  • 50% on next SGD 190,000
  • Max exemption: SGD 102,500/yr
  • No shareholder conditions

4. YA 2025 and YA 2026 corporate tax rebate

On top of SUTE and PTE, the Singapore government has announced corporate tax rebates for both YA 2025 and YA 2026:

Year of AssessmentCIT rebateCash grant
YA 2025 (FY 2024)50% of tax payable, capped at SGD 40,000SGD 2,000 (if employed at least 1 local worker in 2024)
YA 2026 (FY 2025)50% of tax payable, capped at SGD 40,000 or SGD 38,000*SGD 2,000 (if employed at least 1 local worker in 2025)

*Cap is SGD 38,000 for companies that receive the SGD 2,000 cash grant, SGD 40,000 for those that do not. Source: IRAS Budget 2025 and Budget 2026 announcements; PwC Singapore tax summary 2025.

Key point: The CIT rebate is applied after SUTE or PTE. It reduces whatever tax remains after exemptions by 50%, up to the cap. It is applied automatically by IRAS when you file — no separate claim needed. The SGD 2,000 cash grant is non-taxable and paid out separately.

6. Real examples: Wei Ming, Priya, and James

Wei Ming — F&B company, Year 1, profit SGD 80,000

Wei Ming incorporated his catering company in 2024. His first Year of Assessment is YA 2025. With SGD 80,000 in chargeable income, SUTE exempts 75% of the full amount — SGD 60,000 is exempt. Tax on the remaining SGD 20,000 at 17% = SGD 3,400. YA 2025 rebate reduces this by 50% = SGD 1,700 final tax. Plus he receives a SGD 2,000 cash grant since he employed one local staff member.

Effective tax: SGD 1,700 on SGD 80,000 profit — an effective rate of 2.1%. Wei Ming had expected to pay around SGD 13,600 based on the 17% headline rate. The exemptions saved him SGD 11,900 in his first year.

Lesson: In the first 3 years, SUTE combined with the CIT rebate makes Singapore's effective corporate tax rate extremely low for small profitable businesses.

Priya — management consultant, Year 5, profit SGD 250,000

Priya's company is past its SUTE period and now uses PTE. Her chargeable income is SGD 250,000. PTE exempts SGD 102,500 in total. Tax on the remaining SGD 147,500 at 17% = SGD 25,075. YA 2026 rebate of 50% = SGD 12,538. Final tax: approximately SGD 12,537.

Effective rate: SGD 12,537 on SGD 250,000 = 5.0%. Without PTE and the rebate, the tax would have been SGD 42,500. The exemptions saved Priya SGD 29,963 — more than enough to justify keeping clean, accurate accounts throughout the year.

Lesson: Even established companies well past SUTE pay a fraction of the 17% headline rate due to PTE and the annual rebate. Accurate bookkeeping directly translates into lower tax.

James — e-commerce company, Year 2, loss of SGD 15,000

James's company made a loss in its second year. His chargeable income is zero — there is no corporate tax to pay. He still filed Form C-S Lite (his revenue was under SGD 200,000) with a nil tax return, preserving the loss for carry-forward against future profits. He also received the SGD 2,000 cash grant since he employed a part-time local employee.

James almost did not file because he thought "there's nothing to pay, so no need to file." This would have been a mistake — non-filing carries penalties up to SGD 5,000, and the loss carry-forward would not be preserved without a proper return on file.

Lesson: File even when you have no tax to pay. Loss carry-forwards are only available if you filed correctly in the loss year.

7. How chargeable income is calculated

Chargeable income is not the same as accounting profit. You arrive at it through a series of tax adjustments:

  1. Start with accounting profit or loss from your P&L
  2. Add back non-deductible expenses: private car costs, entertainment without business purpose, fines, penalties, and accounting depreciation
  3. Deduct IRAS capital allowances on qualifying plant and machinery (replaces accounting depreciation)
  4. Deduct other allowable deductions: donations to approved institutions (with enhanced deduction), R&D expenditure, and other qualifying costs
  5. Result = adjusted profit, which becomes your chargeable income before exemptions
  6. Apply SUTE or PTE exemptions
  7. Apply the CIT rebate
  8. Result = final tax payable
Common adjustment mistakes: Many small companies forget to add back accounting depreciation and replace it with IRAS capital allowances — which are often more generous. Others forget to add back entertainment expenses that lack a documented business purpose. Both errors result in incorrect chargeable income figures.

8. FAQ

What is the corporate tax rate in Singapore in 2025?
The headline rate is a flat 17%. However, most small companies pay significantly less after SUTE, PTE, and the YA 2025 50% rebate. A qualifying startup with SGD 150,000 chargeable income pays approximately SGD 4,250 — an effective rate of 2.8%.
What is SUTE and who qualifies?
The Start-Up Tax Exemption provides 75% exemption on the first SGD 100,000 and 50% on the next SGD 100,000 of chargeable income for the first 3 Years of Assessment. The company must be Singapore-incorporated, tax-resident, have no more than 20 shareholders, and at least one individual shareholder holding 10% or more. Investment holding and property development companies do not qualify.
What is the YA 2025 corporate tax rebate?
A 50% rebate on corporate tax payable, capped at SGD 40,000. Companies that employed at least one local employee in 2024 also receive a SGD 2,000 non-taxable cash grant. The rebate is applied automatically by IRAS on filing.
What is the difference between SUTE and PTE?
SUTE applies to new companies in their first 3 Years of Assessment and provides larger exemptions (up to SGD 125,000/year). PTE applies to all other companies automatically every year and provides smaller exemptions (up to SGD 102,500/year). Both reduce the effective tax rate well below 17% for most small companies.
Do I pay corporate tax if my company made a loss?
No. If chargeable income is zero or negative, there is no corporate tax. However, you must still file Form C-S or Form C-S Lite by 30 November. Filing a nil return preserves any losses for carry-forward against future profits.
