Every Singapore-incorporated company — regardless of size, revenue, or profit — must file a corporate income tax return with IRAS each year. For small companies, the Form C-S and Form C-S Lite are designed to make this as straightforward as possible. This guide explains exactly who qualifies, what to prepare, and how to file correctly — so you meet your obligations without overpaying or missing the deadline.

1. What is Form C-S?

Form C-S (Companies — Simplified) is IRAS's simplified corporate income tax return for small Singapore companies with straightforward tax affairs. It is a shorter version of the standard Form C, designed to reduce the compliance burden for companies that do not have complex tax structures.

Filing Form C-S does not mean you pay less tax — it simply means you fill in fewer fields. The same corporate tax rate of 17% applies to taxable income regardless of which form you use.

Which form should you use? Start with your annual revenue. If SGD 200,000 or below — check Form C-S Lite eligibility first. If SGD 5 million or below — check Form C-S eligibility. Only use Form C if you cannot qualify for either simplified form or if you have complex tax matters such as capital allowances, group relief, or foreign tax credits.

3. Do you qualify for Form C-S?

Your company qualifies for Form C-S if ALL of the following conditions are met:

  • The company is incorporated in Singapore
  • Annual revenue is SGD 5 million or below
  • The company derives only income taxable at the prevailing 17% corporate rate — no income from exempt sources or at concessionary rates
  • The company does not claim any of the following: carry-back of capital allowances or losses, group relief, investment allowance, foreign tax credit, or tax deducted at source

Your company qualifies for Form C-S Lite if the above conditions are met AND annual revenue is SGD 200,000 or below.

Does not qualify if: Your company received income exempt from tax (such as certain dividends), has foreign-sourced income, claims capital allowances on fixed assets, or has any inter-company transactions requiring transfer pricing documentation. In these cases, you must file Form C instead.

Important: All companies must file by 30 November — including those that had no business activity, made a loss, or had zero revenue. There are no exemptions except for dormant companies that have received an official waiver from IRAS. Filing nil returns is compulsory.
Non-December financial year ends: If your financial year does not end on 31 December, your tax is still assessed in the YA following your financial year end. For example, if your financial year is April 2024 to March 2025, this is assessed in YA 2026 — with a Form C-S deadline of 30 November 2026.

5. ECI — what it is and when to file

ECI (Estimated Chargeable Income) is a separate, earlier filing — a preliminary estimate of your taxable income submitted within 3 months after your financial year ends.

ECI waiver for small companies: If your company's annual revenue is SGD 5 million or below AND your ECI is zero (nil), you do not need to file an ECI. IRAS grants an automatic waiver. This applies to most profitable small companies that have fully offset income with allowable deductions — verify your ECI figure before assuming you are exempt.

Key tax adjustments to calculate

Form C-S requires you to adjust your accounting profit to arrive at taxable income. The most common adjustments for small companies:

  • Add back: non-deductible expenses (private car expenses, fines, penalties, entertainment without business purpose)
  • Add back: depreciation charged in accounts (replace with IRAS capital allowances)
  • Deduct: IRAS capital allowances on qualifying plant and machinery
  • Deduct: startup tax exemption (for companies in their first 3 years of assessment)
  • Deduct: partial tax exemption (for established companies)

7. Real examples: Wei Ming, Priya, and James

Wei Ming — F&B sole director, annual revenue SGD 180,000

Wei Ming runs a small catering business incorporated in Singapore. His annual revenue of SGD 180,000 qualifies him for Form C-S Lite — the simplest possible filing. He only needs to complete six fields: revenue, adjusted profit, capital allowances, chargeable income, and two declaration fields.

Wei Ming's mistake in his first year was filing his accounting profit directly without adjusting for the startup tax exemption. His company was in its second Year of Assessment and entitled to a 75% tax exemption on the first SGD 100,000 of chargeable income. Without the exemption, his tax bill was SGD 6,800. With it, the bill dropped to SGD 1,700 — a difference of SGD 5,100 he almost left on the table.

Lesson: Always check whether your company qualifies for the startup or partial tax exemption. For new companies, the startup exemption alone can save thousands in the first three years.

Priya — management consultant, annual revenue SGD 420,000

Priya's consulting company has been operating for four years. Her revenue of SGD 420,000 qualifies for Form C-S (not Lite). She files herself using myTax Portal each November.

Priya's challenge was understanding which of her expenses were tax-deductible. Her accounting software recorded all expenses — including a SGD 3,200 private car lease and SGD 1,800 in club membership fees. Both are non-deductible under IRAS rules and must be added back to accounting profit before calculating taxable income. Failing to add these back would understate her taxable income.

She also purchased a laptop for SGD 2,800 during the year. Rather than depreciating it over three years in her accounts, she claimed the full SGD 2,800 as a capital allowance in Year 1 under Section 19B accelerated allowance — reducing her taxable income by SGD 2,800 in the current year.

Lesson: Tax adjustments go both ways — add back non-deductible expenses, and claim accelerated capital allowances where available. Both directly affect your tax bill.

James — e-commerce business, missed ECI deadline

James's online retail company had a September financial year end. This meant his ECI was due by 31 December — a deadline he overlooked because he was focused on the November Form C-S deadline from the previous year. IRAS issued an estimated assessment in January based on the previous year's income, which was significantly higher than James's actual profit for the year.

James had to pay the estimated assessment within one month while simultaneously objecting and filing his actual ECI. It took three months to resolve and cost him additional accounting fees. The lesson was expensive: his September financial year end created ECI and Form C-S deadlines that were completely different from the calendar-year deadlines most guides describe.

Lesson: If your financial year does not end in December, map out your specific ECI and Form C-S deadlines on day one. Non-December year ends have different timelines that are easy to miss.

8. How to file on myTax Portal — step by step

1
Log in to myTax Portal
Go to mytax.iras.gov.sg and log in using Corppass. The authorised person must be a director or a tax agent with appropriate access. First-time filers: set up Corppass access before attempting to file.
2
Select the correct form
Navigate to Corporate Tax → File Income Tax Return. The system will guide you to the appropriate form based on your company profile. Confirm whether you are filing Form C-S, Form C-S Lite, or the dormant company form.
3
Enter financial information
Input your revenue, accounting profit or loss, and tax adjustments. The portal provides guidance on each field. For Form C-S Lite, only six fields are required. For Form C-S, you will also complete tax adjustment schedules.
4
Claim applicable exemptions and allowances
Declare your startup tax exemption (first 3 YAs) or partial tax exemption. Enter capital allowances on qualifying assets. The portal calculates the exemption amounts automatically once you enter the chargeable income figure.
5
Review and submit
Review all entries carefully before submitting. Once submitted, amendments require a formal request to IRAS. Print or save the confirmation page — it contains your submission reference number.
6
Pay any tax assessed
IRAS will issue a Notice of Assessment (NOA) after processing your return — typically within a few weeks. Tax must be paid within one month of the NOA date. Pay via GIRO, PayNow Corporate, or internet banking using the reference number on the NOA.

9. Common mistakes and how to avoid them

  • Missing the ECI deadline — if your year-end is not December, map your ECI deadline separately. It is 3 months after financial year end, not November.
  • Not adding back non-deductible expenses — private car, entertainment without business purpose, fines, and penalties must be added back to accounting profit.
  • Forgetting the startup tax exemption — new companies in their first 3 YAs qualify for a 75% exemption on the first SGD 100,000 of chargeable income. This is not automatic — you must declare it.
  • Using accounting depreciation instead of capital allowances — IRAS does not accept accounting depreciation as a deduction. Replace it with IRAS capital allowances on qualifying assets.
  • Assuming nil revenue means no filing required — all companies must file, including dormant ones without a formal IRAS waiver.
  • Filing Form C-S when you should file Form C — if you have foreign income, capital gains, or claim complex reliefs, you must use Form C regardless of revenue size.

10. FAQ

What is the deadline for filing Form C-S in Singapore?
30 November each year. For YA 2026 (financial year 2025), the deadline is 30 November 2026. All companies must file by this date including those with no activity or a loss.
Who qualifies to file Form C-S?
Singapore-incorporated companies with annual revenue of SGD 5 million or below that derive only income taxable at 17% and do not claim complex reliefs such as carry-back losses, group relief, or foreign tax credits. Companies with revenue of SGD 200,000 or below may use the even simpler Form C-S Lite.
What is Form C-S Lite?
A simplified version of Form C-S requiring only six essential fields. Available to companies with annual revenue of SGD 200,000 or below that meet all Form C-S qualifying conditions. Takes approximately 5–10 minutes to complete.
What happens if I miss the Form C-S deadline?
IRAS may issue an estimated assessment and impose a penalty of up to SGD 5,000. You must pay the estimated tax within one month even if you disagree — then object separately. Late payment also attracts a 5% surcharge plus 1% per month.
Do I need to file if my company made a loss?
Yes. All Singapore companies must file a corporate income tax return regardless of profit, loss, or zero activity. The only exception is dormant companies that have received a formal filing waiver from IRAS.
What is ECI and when must I file it?
ECI (Estimated Chargeable Income) is a preliminary tax estimate filed within 3 months after your financial year ends. Companies with annual revenue of SGD 5 million or below and ECI of zero are exempt from filing ECI. Form C-S is filed separately by 30 November with actual figures.
