GST registration is one of the most important compliance milestones for any growing Singapore business. Miss the deadline by even one day and IRAS can backdate your registration — forcing you to pay 9% GST on past sales that you may have already spent. This guide explains exactly when you must register, what has changed in 2025 and 2026, and what happens if you get it wrong.

1. What is GST in Singapore?

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is Singapore's broad-based consumption tax, currently levied at 9% on most goods and services supplied in Singapore and on goods imported into Singapore. The rate was last raised from 8% to 9% on January 1, 2024, and no further rate changes have been announced for 2025 or 2026.

GST-registered businesses act as collection agents for IRAS — charging output tax on sales, claiming input tax credits on qualifying purchases, and remitting the net difference to IRAS quarterly.

2. When must you register? The two tests

IRAS uses two separate tests to determine whether GST registration is compulsory. You must register if you meet either one.

Test 1: Retrospective basis

Look back at your taxable turnover over the past 12 months on a rolling basis. If the total has exceeded SGD 1 million, you are liable to register.

Example: Your taxable turnover from June 2025 to May 2026 totals SGD 1.05 million. You must apply for GST registration by June 30, 2026 (within 30 days of the end of May 2026). Your effective registration date will be August 1, 2026.

Test 2: Prospective basis

At any point in time, if you have reasonable grounds to believe your taxable turnover will exceed SGD 1 million in the next 12 months, you must register. This typically applies when you have signed a large contract or received confirmed purchase orders that will push revenue over the threshold.

2025 update — extended grace period: From July 1, 2025, businesses that trigger the prospective test get a 2-month grace period before GST takes effect (previously it was 31 days). However, you must still apply for registration within 30 days of forming the belief. The grace period only affects when you start charging GST — not when you must apply.
Important: IRAS measures taxable turnover, not total sales. Exempt supplies (residential property, financial services) and out-of-scope supplies are excluded. If you are unsure what counts towards your threshold, this is worth confirming with a bookkeeper before you approach the SGD 1 million mark.

4. Voluntary GST registration

Businesses below the SGD 1 million threshold can choose to register voluntarily. The main reason to do so is to claim input tax credits — reclaiming the 9% GST you pay on qualifying business purchases such as rent, equipment, and professional services.

✓ Voluntary registration makes sense if:
  • You have significant GST-able business expenses
  • Most of your customers are GST-registered businesses (they can claim back the GST you charge)
  • You plan to grow past the threshold soon
  • You export goods or provide international services (zero-rated = GST refund from IRAS)
✗ Voluntary registration may not help if:
  • Most of your customers are individuals who cannot claim input tax
  • You have few GST-able expenses to offset
  • The added compliance burden outweighs the input tax recovery
  • You are not ready to comply with InvoiceNow requirements

Voluntary registrants must remain registered for at least 2 years before applying to deregister. This is a significant commitment — make sure the input tax recovery justifies it before applying.

5. The new InvoiceNow requirement (2025/2026)

This is the most significant change affecting GST registrants in 2025 and 2026. IRAS is phasing in a requirement for all GST-registered businesses to use InvoiceNow-ready accounting software that transmits invoice data digitally via Singapore's Peppol network.

DateWho must comply
November 1, 2025All new voluntary GST registrants (newly incorporated companies)
April 1, 2026All new voluntary GST registrants (all businesses)

Source: IRAS InvoiceNow GST requirement announcement; HeySara SG (April 2026)

What this means in practice: You can no longer issue invoices manually using Word, Excel, or PDF. Your invoicing must be done through InvoiceNow-certified software such as Xero, QuickBooks, or other IRAS-approved platforms. If you are registering for GST voluntarily from November 2025, confirm your accounting software is InvoiceNow-ready before applying.

6. Penalties for late or missed registration

What IRAS can do if you register late
Backdate your GST registration to when you should have registered
Require you to pay 9% GST on all sales from that backdated date — even if you never collected it from customers
Impose a fine of up to SGD 10,000
Charge interest and surcharges on outstanding amounts
Real cost example: If your business should have registered in January 2026 but only registered in July 2026, IRAS may require you to pay 9% GST on every invoice you issued from January to July — money you almost certainly already spent. On SGD 500,000 in sales, that is SGD 45,000 out of pocket.

The most common mistake is focusing only on the calendar year-end figure and missing the rolling 12-month retrospective check. Your taxable turnover should be monitored every month, not just in December.

7. How to register for GST in Singapore

GST registration is done entirely online through IRAS's myTax Portal. The process generally takes 2-3 weeks after submission.

01
Set up Corppass access
A director or authorised representative must have Corppass access to act on the company's GST matters. If not already set up, this is the first step.
02
Complete the IRAS e-Learning course
IRAS requires the company's director or key person to complete a short "Overview of GST" e-learning module and pass a quiz before the application can be submitted.
03
Submit the application via myTax Portal
Log in, navigate to the GST registration section, and select compulsory or voluntary registration. Upload supporting documents including financial statements and revenue records.
04
Set up GIRO for GST payments
IRAS requires GST payments via GIRO (direct debit). Set up a GIRO arrangement with your bank as part of the registration process.
05
Confirm InvoiceNow-ready software
If applicable (voluntary registrants from November 2025, all new registrants from April 2026), ensure your accounting software is InvoiceNow-certified before your registration is effective.

Once approved, IRAS will issue your GST registration number and effective date. Update all your tax invoices to display the GST registration number from the effective date.

8. After registration: your ongoing obligations

  • Charge 9% GST on all standard-rated supplies from your effective registration date
  • Issue tax invoices displaying your GST registration number for all taxable sales above SGD 1,000 (simplified invoices for amounts SGD 1,000 and below)
  • File quarterly GST returns (Form F5) via myTax Portal within one month of each quarter end
  • Keep records for 5 years — all invoices, receipts, and accounting records
  • Monitor your turnover on a rolling 12-month basis to stay compliant

9. FAQ

When must a Singapore business register for GST?
When taxable turnover exceeds SGD 1 million in any rolling 12-month period (retrospective basis), or when you have reasonable grounds to believe it will exceed SGD 1 million in the next 12 months (prospective basis). You must apply within 30 days of becoming liable.
What is the GST rate in Singapore in 2025?
9%, effective from January 1, 2024. No further changes have been announced for 2025 or 2026. Zero-rated supplies (exports and international services) attract 0% GST.
What happens if I don't register for GST on time?
IRAS can backdate your registration and require you to pay 9% GST on all sales from the date you should have been registered — even if you didn't collect it from customers. A fine of up to SGD 10,000 may also apply.
Can I register for GST voluntarily in Singapore?
Yes. Businesses below the SGD 1 million threshold can register voluntarily to claim input tax credits on qualifying expenses. Voluntary registrants must stay registered for at least 2 years and must comply with InvoiceNow requirements.
What is InvoiceNow and does it affect me?
InvoiceNow is Singapore's e-invoicing network using the Peppol standard. From November 2025, new voluntary GST registrants must use InvoiceNow-ready software. From April 2026, this applies to all new registrants. You must use IRAS-approved accounting software — manual Word or Excel invoices are no longer accepted for newly registered businesses.
